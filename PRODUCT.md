# Startups 101: product

**The user.** First-time founders, often students or early-career builders outside startup hubs, with an idea and
no network: no one to show them what a pitch memo looks like or which questions to ask customers.

**The painful job.** Turning "I want to start something" into concrete first steps, and producing the documents
that make an idea legible to co-founders, early users, mentors and (sometimes) investors.

**The promise.** In nine short modules, learn each stage by doing it: every module ends with a document you fill in
and keep. Free, no sign-up, nothing leaves your browser.

**The wedge.** Search for the documents founders need ("pitch memo template", "MVP plan template", "SAFE
calculator", "customer discovery questions") lands on a working builder, and the builder leads into the module
that explains it. Answer engines can cite module pages, which are plain, specific and sourced.

**Why now.** Free alternatives teach by video and essay; the do-the-work format (fill in the document as you
learn) is rarer, and the old cohort courses that did it have closed or gone paid.

**Activation metric.** A learner completes their first document (the +100 XP event), measurable with the
GoatCounter key as visits to the progress page after a builder page; per-event counts are not built yet.

**First revenue event.** None on Pages by design. Later options (OPERATIONS.md, key 2): a link to a founder
program, donations, or paid reviewed certificates on another host.

**What v1 deliberately doesn't do.** Accounts, cohorts, mentor or AI review of submissions, certificates, funding
eligibility, video, community features. No income or funding promises, ever.
